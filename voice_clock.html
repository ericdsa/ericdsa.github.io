VOICE ALARM CLOCK A DIFFERENT WAY OF WAKING UP  
Most people start off their day waking up to a blaring alarm clock. Startled, their first reaction is to shut it off and return to sleep. And this is how we start our day. Our very first moment we experience in the day is that of irritation, and it shouldn't be that way. I explore other ways that we might wake up, both to better wake us up, and to start our day on a better note. Tools: Pen + Paper, Voice, Adobe Illustrator IDEATION We started ideation considering any device that could wake someone up in the morning. Common electronic devices include smartphones, voice assistants, and standalone clocks. We also considered mechanical contraptions that would wake someone up, from fixtures that would nudge your mattress in the morning, to pillows that would vibrate at a specified time.

We narrowed down our options to a smartphone app, which would be most easily accessible, and a voice assistant, which would be an experimental approach. REFLECTIONS FROM IDEATION The alarm rings, we wake up, lazily snooze it, and go back to sleep. This happens every day, we become habituated to it. However, when different events happens from our routine, we excitedly wake up, because of the novelty. Our alarms should not be repetitive either, and this was the primary motivating factor behind our approach to a new alarm clock.

There are two main pain points at the moment we first wake up:

1) Habituation: we get used to the alarm sound, and don't react as much to it
2) Irritation: The blaring sound of an alarm annoys us, and the quickest way to comfort is to snooze and go back to bed. The blaring sound is the way we start our day; in other words, we start irritated.

In each of these cases, the incentive is only to shut the alarm off, not get out of bed and start the day. However, when we have one-off events/celebrations, we're excited to get out of bed, often earlier than needed.

Many alarm clocks lack daily novelty and a reason to start the day. They seek to annoy you to remove the problem, and hopefully wake up by the time the problem is addressed.

We want to address this problem by changing the challenges daily for novelty. We also aim to create situations that excite users in the morning. EVALUATION OF IDEATION CONCEPTS Before deciding the interface we would use, we decided to narrow down on what the alarm clock would do. Then, we could decide the interface best suited for the function. We decided on three possible functions:

1) Conversational: talk about exciting events in your day. This depends on having an easily accessible calendar, but a natural interface for humans.
2) Daily Challenges: smartphone app that changes the challenge you do every day. You don't know the challenge until the alarm rings in the morning. This works with success in a variety of situations, but heavily depends upon new activities constantly being made, or the novelty disappears.

3) Planner: schedule an exciting event in the morning, then remind you of it later. This is a very effective strategy, but requires one to think every night of an event to schedule an event for the next day.
We decided on the 1st, a conversational interface. This would mean either a chatbot on a smartphone or a voice assistant. We chose the latter, since it was most suited for conversations and felt more natural. SCRIPT V1 For our first script, we drew inspiration from the most effective alarm clock we knew - our parents. In the general form, someone wakes you up by shaking you, followed by a call to action: "You're late for school!" We made our first design considering this analogy of a voice waking you up.

The main challenge in this version was whether the user should set an alarm for multiple days or just one. Multiple days would be much more complex. Additionally, when people get into bed and think about when to wake up, they're normally only thinking about when to wake up the very next day, and not any day after that. Thus, we narrowed down our use case to setting an alarm for the next day. TESTING + SCRIPT V2 Our main feedback from the first script was that most people take a few seconds to remember their events in the next day. To reduce this friction, we considered a calendar import, to make it easier to check the events happening the next day.

We didn't want the alarm clock to automatically set alarms based on the calendar; we wanted to retain user verification and input. For our second version, we added a calendar read function. The alarm clock will read events happening the next day for the user. However, the user still tells the alarm clock what event and time to set the alarm clock for. SCRIPT V3 Our feedback was primarily of issues with the flow of the script:

1) Removed Setup: most people set an alarm day by day, not regularly. We had kept a setup for a repeating alarm as a legacy feature - we removed it after this iteration.
2) Intents: To generalize the script for any time and/or event, we added intents and variables for time and event (inspired by the Alexa Skills Kit). For example, "Set my alarm at {timeToSet} for {eventName}."
3) Confirmations: To double check the alarm clock heard user input correctly, it reads our user input in a confirmation sentence before proceeding to the next task. SCRIPT V4 This version had very few modifications. In particular, sometimes, users would add alarms, but couldn't delete them afterwards, which we fixed in this version.
