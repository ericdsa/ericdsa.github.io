A Fortune 500 company approached the Haas Innovation Analytics Group with a problem they were facing with customer retention. They wanted to use analytics to uncover patterns and develop a possible solution. As a research assistant in the group, I helped start the analysis of this unstructured data. Because this project involved strategy decisions with a private Fortune 500 company, some details are under NDA, feel free to reach out out me if you want to hear more about this project and my contributions! Team: Haas Innovation Analytics
Tools: Python, C, z-label LDA PROBLEM SPACE This firm (will refer to as the Firm since their name is protected under NDA) had a guess that their customers were not buying everything they could at the Firm's stores. The Firm specialized in do-it-yourself projects. Here is an example of their thought process. A customer walks into Store A to collect ingrediants to bake cookies. They have a list of things they need to bake cookies: dough, baking sheet, aluminum foil, and eggs. They buy dough, a baking sheet, and aluminum foil from Store A, but do not buy eggs. Store A's guess is that the customer possibly bought eggs from somewhere else, instead of at their store. The Firm wanted their customers to buy everything they need for their projects solely from their store. In summary, they wanted to infer the customer's intent or planned project from their purchases, then stock on items that they thought the customer was not purchasing from them. SOLUTION We chose to approach this problem with topic modeling, a branch of natural language procesing. Topic modeling is a group of algorithms and models that attempts to infer a topic from text. For our analysis, we chose Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA). In LDA, each topic is modeled as a probability distribution of words. As a block of text is scanned, each word is added to the topic(s) that it comes under. After LDA finishes, the topic with the highest frequency of words is returned as the topic of the text. This worked well for identifying topics within customers' transaction data. However, the problem with this is that the results of the analysis included topics that don't necessarily lead to projects (e.g. toasting bread). In other words, the list of projects someone can take on is constrained, and thus, the list of topics is constrained. Thus, we adapted our approach to use a variation of LDA, z-label LDA, that would be semi-supervised instead of unsupervised. In this variation, there is a constraint on the topics, and words read in a text only get matched to distributions of the specified topics; if there is no match, they are thrown out of the total analysis. To run this variation, we ingested a list of common projects that one would go to the Firm for. Then, we applied topic modeling to customer transactions with these projects in mind.
