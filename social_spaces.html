UC BERKELEY SOCIAL SPACES LIFESTYLYE CHANGES FOR STUDENTS  
This project originates from UGBA 190T (Needfinding), a class offered at UC Berkeley that is a replica of the class taught by Michael Barry at Stanford's d.school. Our capstone project was a user research project, investigating how students cope, stay sane, or thrive at UC Berkeley. Collaborators: Chris Poulsen, Jocelyn Nguyen, Marcus van Duren
Tools: Adobe Illustrator, Interviewing, 2x2 Frameworks INTERVIEWING: ROUND 1 For our first round of interviews, we kept our questions very general and open-ended. Although we had our own guesses based on our experiences as UC Berkeley students, we didn't want to assume that was the experience of every person. Thus, our questions were mostly introductive, asking for people's lifestyles as students. Key things included their sleeping and eating patterns, where they studied, places they went when they were stressed, and when they were around people vs being alone. Our main conclusions from this round of interviews was that most people recognized their stress points highest when they were studying at the library. This also stemmed from them reaching out to other people to study with them, to alleviate their stress. In other words, having people around them while they studied made their work fun instead of stressful. For our next round of interviews, we decided to focus on friends and support groups as ways of handling stress.
