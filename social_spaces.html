went through them later for writing down stories + insights INTERVIEWING: ROUND 1 For our first round of interviews, we kept our questions very general and open-ended. Although we had our own guesses based on our experiences as UC Berkeley students, we didn't want to assume that was the experience of every person.

Thus, our questions were mostly introductive, asking for people's lifestyles as students. Key things included their sleeping and eating patterns, where they studied, places they went when they were stressed, and when they were around people vs being alone.

Our main conclusions from this round of interviews was that most people recognized their stress points highest when they were studying at the library. This also stemmed from them reaching out to other people to study with them, to alleviate their stress. In other words, having people around them while they studied made their work fun instead of stressful. For our next round of interviews, we decided to focus on friends and support groups as ways of handling stress. INTERVIEWING: ROUND 2 Our second round of interviews focused on friendships that people made during their college experience, and how those friendships were formed. Interviewees were mostly grouped into two main categories:

1) Random Occurences: People who met their closest friends through seemingly random occurences, from sharing cookies on a dorm floor to mutual friends while house hunting.
2) Consistent Occurences: People who met their friends through consistent, frequently occuring activities. In this group, people bonded over a long period of time, reinforced by regular meetings.

Creating a solution to help people meet through random occurences was a formidable task, and we weren't confident it would be effective. We chose to focus on people who formed their friendships through meeting particular people on a regular basis. INTERVIEWING: ROUND 3 Our final round of interviews was purely focused on friendships formed by consistent meetings with other people. Our results were that consistent, easily accessible activities are the most effective for helping people bond. People get recommendations from friends on activities to do, and as they spend time together doing activities, people form close connections. INTERVIEW ANALYSIS We rewatched all our interviews, and added to our insights + notes for each interview. Then, we grouped our insights into categories, and played around with them to find what we could do to address people forming close friendships at school.

Process of finding activities is hard, mostly through random occurences applies to people of all years, not just freshmen

Current spaces on campus are open, flexible spaces with no intended purpose. People congregate and spend time together, but they need a reason to come together, they won't blindly sit down somewhere and spend time together (culture of always doing something / being productive). Most of the time, people come together to study (being productive + being with people). Campus spaces are open-ended, leading to more academic work, less of other activities. 1ST PROTOTYPE Section Content

Originating Thought: when freshmen meet each other for the first time on campus, they often add each other on Facebook. But then they never see each other or reach out to each other again.

Awkward conversation 3 years later when you run into them in a class: "Hey I know you! We met in the dining hall in our first semester!"

Goal: reinforce virtual friendships on Facebook in real life

Concept Tested:
1) Enter a university building
2) Use an app to view friends in the building and the activities they're doing
3) Join an activity that you want with other people
Some inspiration with edits from Facebook check in:
You check in and say you went to a place. However, most people see that hours or days later, at home, on their phone or laptop. There's no call to action, meaning you saw someone went to a place, you spend a second thinking about it, and then you move on with your life.

What we would love with check in is for other people you want to meet to see that you're checking in to a place, so that they can JOIN you, and not just see that you entered a place. In a general environment, this might end up being privacy weird, because then you'd basically know where everyone is all the time.

To modify this, we only allowed people to see where other people are in a building that they are in. Meaning when you enter a building, you only see the people in that building, in that moment in time, and nothing outside of that.

Solving the case of finding things to do in the same palce with an activity in mind. E.g. you enter a library to study, you spend 3 seconds looking around for people you know. If you find someone, great! If you don't, you sit alone somewhere. However, it's possible someone you know is there, but you just didn't see them for whatever reason. We want to fix that problem.
