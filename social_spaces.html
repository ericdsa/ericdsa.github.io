PROJECTS EXPERIENCE RESUME ABOUT ERIC D'SA
UC BERKELEY SOCIAL SPACES LIFESTYLYE CHANGES FOR STUDENTS  
This project originates from UGBA 190T (Needfinding), a class offered at UC Berkeley that is a replica of the class taught by Michael Barry at Stanford's d.school. Our capstone project was a user research project, investigating how students cope, stay sane, or thrive at UC Berkeley. Collaborators: Chris Poulsen, Jocelyn Nguyen, Marcus van Duren
Tools: Adobe Illustrator, Interviewing, 2x2 Frameworks INTERVIEW GUIDELINES + PROCESS How we interviewed - in person, across the table, conversational What kind of questions did we ask how long were interviews what did we gain from the interviews what insights we wrote down after interviews recorded videos and saw them later to look for additional insights INTERVIEWING: ROUND 1 For our first round of interviews, we kept our questions very general and open-ended. Although we had our own guesses based on our experiences as UC Berkeley students, we didn't want to assume that was the experience of every person. Thus, our questions were mostly introductive, asking for people's lifestyles as students. Key things included their sleeping and eating patterns, where they studied, places they went when they were stressed, and when they were around people vs being alone. Our main conclusions from this round of interviews was that most people recognized their stress points highest when they were studying at the library. This also stemmed from them reaching out to other people to study with them, to alleviate their stress. In other words, having people around them while they studied made their work fun instead of stressful. For our next round of interviews, we decided to focus on friends and support groups as ways of handling stress. INTERVIEWING: ROUND 2 Our second round of interviews focused on friendships that people made during their college experience, and how those friendships were formed. Interviewees were mostly grouped into two main categories: 1) Random Occurences: People who met their closest friends through seemingly random occurences, from sharing cookies on a dorm floor to mutual friends while house hunting. 2) Consistent Occurences: People who met their friends through consistent, frequently occuring activities. In this group, people bonded over a long period of time, reinforced by regular meetings. Creating a solution to help people meet through random occurences was a formidable task, and we weren't confident it would be effective. We chose to focus on people who formed their friendships through meeting particular people on a regular basis. INTERVIEWING: ROUND 3 Our final round of interviews was purely focused on friendships formed by consistent meetings with other people. Our results were that consistent, easily accessible activities are the most effective for helping people bond. People get recommendations from friends on activities to do, and as they spend time together doing activities, people form close connections. INTERVIEW ANALYSIS We rewatched all our interviews, and added to our insights + notes for each interview. Then, we grouped our insights into categories, and played around with them to find what we could do to address people forming close friendships at school.
